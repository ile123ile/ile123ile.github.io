Eli Goldin

I am currently a postdoctoral research scientist with the Princeton Quantum Initiative working with Alex Lombardi. Previously, I completed my PhD studying cryptography at NYU advised by Marshall Ball and Yevgeniy Dodis.

My classical research mainly aims to answer the question: "how do you do cryptography when the cryptographers are out to get you?" Security practitioners often use cryptographic primitives in a black-box manner. But sometimes, these black boxes are themselves malicious. This may be caused by a backdoor inserted by some third party or just an unseen attack. I care about provably helping practitioners stuck in such a hopeless situation recover some security.

In the quantum setting, my research focuses on the foundations of quantum cryptography. I care about fundamentally quantum primitives, which require quantum computers to even execute. I am interested in the necessary assumptions for achieving quantum cryptography, and mapping out the relationships between quantum primitives. I have a particular interest in the "MicroCrypt" setting, where one may hope to achieve useful cryptography using quantum computers even if one-way functions and all of classical cryptography do not exist.

I am also generally interested in the theory of cryptography, in particular randomness extraction and quantum cryptography.

Game Dev

I make video games in my free time. Here are some projects I've worked on:
  • My biggest project, Herman Electro: Explore six puzzle-packed floors and help Herman and his friends reach the outside world. A circuit-based puzzle roguelike. Available on Steam!
  • Hearsay: A daily word game where you solve a jigsaw puzzle made out of a famous quote.
  • Spooky Caverns: With a ghost hot on your heels in the Spooky Caverns, your chances of escape are slim. You'll need to run to the right, pick up powerful items, and find enough keys to unlock a portal out of the depths. A Spooky 2D Jam '23 entry.
  • Become Tree: start as a small tree, grow your roots, and become a much bigger tree. A Global Game Jam 2023 entry.
  • Time to Work: a story about time travel and mundane mistakes.
  • Date at the Museum: a two-player game where you predict how your partner would respond to a prompt.
  • Synesthesia Spectrum: a subaquatic race to the finish. Flip gravity and mess with your opponent.
